Clinch Bluff

The last lakefront land in Hardin Valley, finished as twenty private homesites.

We plan to buy 44.45 acres with about 1,200 feet of bluff above Melton Hill Lake, build a gated private road, bring water, power and wastewater to every lot, and sell twenty finished homesites in three tiers. The thesis

Hardin Valley has run out of lakefront, and finished lots here sell for a premium

Williams Bend is the last lake property in Hardin Valley, the most exclusive part of Knoxville right now. Buyers already pay estate prices on this road for small houses and bare lots. We turn 44 raw acres into twenty lots a buyer can build on the day they close.

The last

Lake property in Hardin Valley

On October 24, 2026 this tract was one of only two land listings in the Hardin Valley ZIP with any lake frontage, and the other cannot take a dock. Hardin Valley lakefront homes have risen 84% to 148% since 2020.

$1.25M

Appraisal on a 1,200 SF house

A neighboring Williams Bend home bought off-market for $950,000 appraised at $1.25 million during escrow. Buyers here are paying for the address and the land.

$550K

A 3-acre lot with no lake view

Another Williams Bend lot without a view is going for $550,000 as raw land, with no utilities in. Our finished, gated Regular lots launch above it at $625,000.

$1.3M–$2.45M

Finished lakefront lots in Arcadia

Five lakefront homesites in Arcadia, Knoxville’s gated community on Fort Loudoun Lake, closed between $1.3 million and $2.45 million from 2024 to 2026. Current Arcadia lakefront lots ask up to $3.0 million.

Why a finished lot is worth more than raw land

A buyer who purchases raw lakefront acreage still has to build a road, extend water and power, get septic approved and wait a year or more before starting a house. Our buyers skip all of it. Water and power are at the lot line, the wastewater system is in, the road is paved and the gate is up, so they close and start building. That certainty is what supports $1 million-plus pricing for the Riverfront lots.

Approvals

Twenty lots are allowed by right

The A zone requires one acre per home, and 20 lots on 44.45 acres is less than half that density, so no rezoning is needed. Knox County approval runs through three steps, about 16 to 26 months from closing to recorded lots.

Pre-application and diligence

Meet Knox Planning, County Engineering and the Health Department. Complete the topo survey, a full high-intensity soil map, the stream determination, and West Knox Utility District and LCUB service letters.

6 to 12 months

Final Plat

Planning staff certify the plat with Health Department sign-off, and lots can close. A recent Williams Bend Rd plat took about 10 weeks.

About 10 weeks

The private street

A private road for six or more lots is built to county street standards: a 40 to 50 ft right-of-way, 20 to 26 ft of pavement and grades up to 12%, maintained by the HOA. The gate is approved as a condition of the plan.

Septic sets the final count

About 30 acres sit under the 30% slope limit for septic, roughly 1.2 usable acres per lot after the road and buffers. The full soil map confirms each lot before closing on the land, with drip systems, a community system or a sewer extension as fallbacks.

Greenbelt rollback

About $10,000 to $13,000, triggered as each phase is platted. Keeping the land in Greenbelt during approvals defers it.

Risks and how we manage them

What could change the numbers

Lot count

Zoning allows 20 lots by right, but each lot needs enough suitable soil for its septic system and a duplicate area. If parts of the site map poorly the count could fall into the mid teens. We run the full soil map during the diligence period, before closing, and the model shows the result at any lot count.

Wastewater

There is no public sewer. Soils mapped in 2020 support subsurface systems at the sites tested. We map every planned lot before closing and keep a community sewer system as the alternative, and both are priced in the model.

Water access

The shoreline below the bluff is TVA habitat-protection land, and TVA does not allow docks there. Every price in this plan assumes no dock. Riverfront lots are sold on the bluff, the view and the protected shoreline, with public boat ramps about 1.3 miles away.

Site costs

The budget uses our own contractor pricing, well below published East Tennessee rates, and a 10% contingency. The road drops about 100 to 160 feet from Williams Bend Rd to the bluff, and rock can raise grading costs, so we get an early road profile and lock fixed prices with our subcontractors before closing.

Sales pace and price

Reservations during construction test pricing before the lots are finished. Holding the best Riverfront lots for later releases protects price, and the model shows profit at slower sales and lower prices.

Gate and street connections

The Planning Commission can ask for a stub street to neighboring land, which works against a gate. We settle the gate and any connection request at the pre-application meeting.

Interest rates

The loan covers 65% of cost, and lot sales repay it first. The sensitivity chart shows the effect of a two-point rate change.
