Riverfront bluff homesites at Clinch Bluff support $1.1 million at launch

A buyer at Clinch Bluff gets something no one else can sell them: a finished, gated homesite on the last lakefront land in Hardin Valley, with the road paved, water and power in, and a septic system installed. Three independent methods put the Riverfront tier between $1.0 million and $1.9 million before any dock value. Docks are not possible on this shoreline, so we price below that range: launch at $1.1 million and step up to $1.35 million for the final bluff lots.

Three ways to price the tiers

Each method lands at or above our launch prices

  1. Start from the Williams Bend lot and apply the market’s tier premiums

    In small gated lake enclaves, waterfront lots sell for about 2.3 to 3.5 times interior lots, and view lots for about 1.1 to 1.6 times. Applied to the $550,000 no-view lot on Williams Bend, that puts Riverfront at $1.27 million to $1.93 million and Lake View at $605,000 to $880,000. Most of those waterfront lots include a dock, so our $1.1 million Riverfront launch sits below the range on purpose.

  2. Compare to Knoxville’s finished gated lakefront

    Five Arcadia lakefront homesites closed between $1.3 million and $2.45 million from September 2024 to August 2026, and current Arcadia lakefront lots ask $2.25 million to $3.0 million. Arcadia view lots without frontage closed at $750,000 and $975,000. Across the region, 14 lakefront lots have closed above $1 million since late 2023.

  3. Work back from the home a buyer will build

    In Arcadia and Rarity Bay the lot has run 15% to 29% of the home later built on it. A $1.1 million Riverfront lot fits a $3.8 million to $7.3 million home, a level Knoxville’s top tier now reaches regularly. Four Knoxville-area homes closed between $8.15 million and $10.5 million in 2026, and Arcadia lots that sold for $1.2 million in 2019 now trade at $2.1 million to $2.45 million.

Why Regular lots launch at $625,000, above the raw Williams Bend lot

A 3-acre lot on Williams Bend with no view is going for $550,000 as raw land. Its buyer still has to pay for a pump or drip septic system ($20,000 to $25,000 on these soils), a power run from LCUB, a water tap and line, and the soil map, and wait 6 to 12 months before starting a house. That is roughly $40,000 to $75,000 of cost plus the risk that the soils fail. Our Regular buyer gets all of it done, behind a gate on an HOA-maintained road, next to $1 million Riverfront lots. Plain, ungated 5-acre lots at Cornish Meadows in Lenoir City sold for $597,500 to $710,000, about 18 of them in 18 months. Our lots are about 2 acres, which is why we stop at $625,000 to $675,000.

Water access, stated plainly

Protected shoreline below the bluff, and no docks

The Riverfront lots run down the bluff to the TVA line, which sits only 10 to 45 feet from the water. That strip is TVA Melton Hill Parcel 38, known as Hewitt Bluff, a habitat protection area where TVA does not allow docks, slips or clearing.

We put no dock value in any price on this page. The Riverfront tier is priced as bluff-front view homesites, which is why it launches below the lakefront comparables that come with docks.

Views no one can block

TVA land below the lots stays wild. No neighbor can build, clear or put in a dock between a Riverfront home and the river.

Trails on your own land

Owners can build paths, stairs and overlooks on their lots down to the TVA line.

Boating close by

The public ramp at Melton Hill Park is about 1.3 miles away, and a marina is about 3.3 miles away.

How fast they sell

About two years from the first closing to the last

Launch with reservations

Small gated enclaves typically place 3 to 5 lots under reservation before the plat records. At The Cliffs on Lake Keowee, 5 of 26 lakefront homesites went under contract on the first release weekend.

Then about two a quarter

After launch, about two closings a quarter sells all 20 lots in roughly 24 to 30 months. Cornish Meadows in Lenoir City sold about 18 plain five-acre lots at $597,500 to $710,000 in 18 months, and Saylors Watch on Lake Norman sold all 11 of its gated waterfront lots.

A community sells faster than a single lot

Single Knoxville lots listed one at a time have taken 300 to 500 days at $750,000 and up. A branded, gated launch with a builder program gives buyers a reason to act together.

What could hold price down

The questions a buyer’s agent will ask, and our answers

Can I put in a dock?

No. The shoreline is TVA habitat-protection land, and TVA does not consider docks there. Every price on this page already assumes no dock. The upside is that the view and the shoreline below can never be built on.

Haven’t lake lots softened in 2026?

Several new East Tennessee lake offerings cut prices this year. That is why the plan opens at the base price, uses reservations to prove demand, and steps up only after real closings.

Melton Hill hasn’t seen $1 million lots

Every $1 million-plus lot sale so far has been on Fort Loudoun, the Tennessee River or Tellico, because no one has finished and gated a Melton Hill bluff like this. Raw Melton Hill tracts sell in the $300,000s for the same reason. The Hardin Valley lakefront homes that have more than doubled since 2020 show the buyers are already here.

Will the lots appraise?

Most buyers at this level pay cash for the lot. For buyers who finance, the Williams Bend sale that appraised $300,000 over its price and the Arcadia closings give an appraiser real support.

What if values keep running?

Planned land in Hardin Valley has re-traded fast. A 59-acre tract across from the Hardin Valley schools sold for $2.7 million in 2023 and again for $10.3 million in 2025. The release plan steps prices up as closings confirm the market, so the project captures that rise.
